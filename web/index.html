    Why raw weights can't be compared

    Take any trained network and swap two hidden neurons, along with their incoming and outgoing weights. Nothing about its behaviour changes, yet the weight matrices now look different. A transformer has far more of these free choices: the neuron order in every MLP, the order of attention heads, an arbitrary basis inside each head, and a rotation of the whole residual stream when there is no LayerNorm to pin it. Two networks can be the same machine and still have raw weight cosine near zero.

    So Isomorph compares in steps, and each step removes one kind of freedom.

    The ladder

    1. Raw weights. Cosine similarity of the parameters as stored.
    2. Weight symmetries removed. B is rewritten in A's coordinates: one orthogonal rotation of the residual stream (fit by Procrustes on activations), a head permutation (matched on the basis-free circuits WQWKT and WVWO), an orthogonal basis inside each head, and a neuron permutation (Hungarian matching on activation correlation). The app checks that B's outputs did not move.
    3. Task symmetries removed. The problem itself has symmetries. For addition mod p, multiplying every number by a unit u gives another valid way to compute the same thing, and it turns a clock circuit at frequency k into one at frequency k·u. So two models that picked different frequencies can still be built from identical parts. The app relabels B's inputs and matches its circuits one frequency at a time, against a null of relabellings that should not line up.

    Functional tests

    Linear interpolation walks the straight line from A's weights to B's. A flat path means they sit in the same basin; a bump means the path crosses a region of high loss. Doing it after alignment tests whether the symmetries were the only thing separating them.

    Stitching takes A's internal state at some point, maps it into B's space, and lets B finish the computation. If B still gets the right answer, the two models were carrying the same information at that point. Note that stitching at the token embedding is almost always easy, since a handful of tokens in a wide space can be mapped anywhere.

    CKA and mutual nearest neighbours ask whether two layers organise the inputs the same way, ignoring rotation and scale.

    Where the savings would come from

    If different runs keep converging to the same parts, those parts are worth knowing in advance. The compression probe in the lab shows how few embedding directions a trained model actually needs. On real models, a low-rank difference between a base and its fine-tune means the update could have been trained and stored as a small adapter instead of a full copy.

    Related projects

    Laya ships a base checkpoint and a fine-tuned one with the same encoder, which makes it a natural base-versus-fine-tune pair for the weight comparison. Colibri is an inference engine for very large mixture-of-experts models; its measured expert atlas is a different angle on the same question, showing that routing inside one model has stable, cacheable structure.
